I'm a **technology professional with a background in industrial engineering and software development**, currently focusing on **cybersecurity, particularly Blue Team and defensive security**.

Throughout my career, I've worked on solving problems and building solutions in both the physical and digital worlds — from industrial systems, automation, and real-world processes to software applications and digital products.

More than 20 years of experience have taken me across different fields, technologies, and ways of approaching problems. What has remained constant is my curiosity and my desire to understand how things work, find solutions, and keep exploring what's possible.

## Who am I?

My name is Enrique, and I've always been curious about understanding how things work — whether it's a physical system, an industrial process, or a piece of software.

My journey started in industrial engineering and process automation, working with systems and solutions connected to the physical world. That experience taught me to look at problems from an engineering perspective: understand the environment, analyse how its components interact, and find practical ways to make things work better.

Over time, that curiosity led me into software development. I've worked on backend applications, mainly within the Java and Kotlin ecosystems, developed Android applications, and built products of my own. I've enjoyed both designing digital solutions and dealing with the practical challenges of turning ideas into something real.

Moving between these worlds has given me a varied perspective on technology. I've seen how physical systems, software, people, and processes depend on each other — and how important it is to understand the whole picture rather than just individual components.

What has remained constant throughout this journey is my interest in understanding what happens behind the scenes, figuring out why things work — or why they don't — and finding ways to make them better.

## A new direction: Cybersecurity

More recently, I've decided to take my career in a new direction and focus on cybersecurity, particularly defensive security and the Blue Team.

I'm interested in understanding how systems fail, how threats develop, how attacks can be detected, and what can be done to make systems more resilient.

I'm currently building practical experience through hands-on labs and personal projects, exploring areas such as security monitoring with Wazuh, vulnerability management, system hardening, threat detection, and incident investigation.

I'm also curious about areas where software development and security meet, including Software Supply Chain Security, AI Security, and binary analysis. These are topics I want to explore further as I continue developing my skills.

I'm still at the beginning of this professional transition, but I'm bringing with me a solid technical background, years of experience solving complex problems, and the motivation to apply that experience to a new field.

## Software, AI and engineering

Although my focus is shifting towards cybersecurity, software development is still a big part of what I enjoy doing.

I continue to code, experiment with tools, build small projects, and write scripts to automate tasks or investigate ideas. For me, programming has never been just about writing code. It's a way to understand problems, explore possibilities, and turn ideas into working solutions.

I've also completed a Master's degree in AI Development, which gave me the opportunity to explore modern AI technologies, tools, and development approaches in depth.

I'm particularly interested in how AI is changing the way we build software and solve problems, but also in the new challenges it brings from a security perspective.

I see AI as another tool in the engineer's toolbox — a powerful one, certainly, but one that needs to be understood and used thoughtfully.

## My approach to technology

I don't like defining myself by a particular programming language, framework, or tool. Technologies change, and new ones appear all the time. What matters to me is understanding the fundamentals and knowing how to apply them to real problems.

I enjoy working at the intersection of ideas and reality: understanding a problem, designing a solution, and seeing it take shape — whether through hardware, industrial systems, software, or a combination of them.

I like exploring how systems are designed, how their components interact, how information flows between them, and what happens when something goes wrong.

I also believe that some of the best learning happens by doing: experimenting, breaking things, investigating why they broke, and trying again.

**Tools evolve. Principles remain.**

## This blog

This blog is my personal space to document what I'm learning, share things I find interesting, and explore ideas that catch my attention.

You'll find notes and experiments around software development, cybersecurity, AI, automation, and technology in general. Some posts may be detailed technical explorations; others might simply be thoughts, discoveries, or things I don't want to forget.

I don't intend this to be a polished collection of tutorials or a showcase of things I've already mastered. It's more of a learning journal — a place to experiment, make mistakes, connect ideas, and share the journey.

I believe the best way to understand something is to get your hands dirty.
